## Running Lingo

As with most sports or industries, the sport of running has its own lingo that as a coach you need to be familiar with.

**5K:** Common distance of a running race (3.1 miles)

**10K:** Common distance of running race (6.2 miles)

**13.1:** Distance in miles of a half-marathon

**26.2:** Distance in miles of a marathon

**AG:** Age Group

**Aid Station:** Locations that are spaced throughout a race where participants can receive fuel, hydration and first aid

**AT:** Anaerobic Threshold

**Bonk:** Also called "hitting the wall". Occurs when the glycogen stores in the body are empty and the athlete suffers extreme fatigue and the body's energy source essentially shuts down.

**Chip:** An electronic chip that tracks the athlete throughout the race

**Chute:** Sometimes called the "finishing chute". This is the area directly after the end of the race that athletes funnel into

**Corral:** Designated areas where runners line up prior to the start of a race. Usually denoted by a runner's estimated pace

**Draft:** To run behind another person to save energy

**Dropped:** If a runner cannot keep up with a group of runners and has been left behind, they have been "dropped"

**DNF:** Did Not Finish

**DNS:** Did Not Start

**DQ:** Disqualified

**Fartlek:** Type of running workout that integrates random speed changes

**Gait:** Denotes the form of a runner and in particular, the legs

**Half:** Half distance marathon (13.1 miles)

**HR:** Heart rate

**HRM:** Heart rate monitor

**Kick:** Used to describe the sprint to the finish line

**Leg Turnover:** Used to denote the running stride speed (i.e. fast, slow)

**LT:** Lactate Threshold

**Marshall:** Individuals on the course to ensure the safety of the participants as well as to ensure all participants follow the rules

**Pace:** Denotes min/mile rate for a set duration of time or distance

**Pack:** Group of runners

**Pick Ups:** Short bursts of speed or accelerations during a run

**Plyos:** Short for "Plyometrics", meaning explosive, jumping style movements. Meant to build power

**PR:** Personal Record (i.e. best time at a specific distance event)

**Repeats:** A type of interval. Repeated up tempo efforts (typically of the same distance) with a rest period in between

**Strides:** Short sprints often done prior to, or after a run

**Tempo:** A steady-state effort often associated with running at a pace close to one's LT.

**VO2:** Commonly refers to the "VO2 Max Test or one's aerobic capacity based on the test

**VT:** Ventilatory Threshold

**Wave:** Large running events often combine runners of similar paces in a group, called a 'wave.' Waves are often broken down further into 'corrals.'

## Running Etiquette

- During track workouts, if recovering or running slowly, stay to outside lanes
- If getting water or food at aid stations during a race, quickly select items but do not stop in this area. Keep it moving.
- If not getting food or water at aid stations, run on the far left to make room for those that are getting food or water
- At the finish line, do not stop at the line. Keep moving through to not clog up the finish area
- If walking during a race, stay to the far right of the road

### General

- Don't litter
- Do not urinate in public. Use porta-potty's and, or bathrooms allocated by the event for use
- If waiting in line for a porta-potty, allow participants with earlier start times than you ahead of you
- Be on time for group training sessions
- Respect and follow the workout type for group training sessions
- No swearing at event
- Thank the race director, sponsors and volunteers at events
- Cheer on other participants
